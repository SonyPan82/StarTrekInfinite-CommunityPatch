Goal
----
Reduce late-game ship object spam by pushing the game toward compact military
forces, around 20 non-hero military ships per empire where possible, while
keeping the result coherent for AI empires, contenders, crises, factions and
scripted fleets.

STATUS 2026-05-14 — hard cap at shipyard (canonical: Mod info/CP26_SHIP_FLEET_CAP_SYSTEM_2026-05-14.txt)
  The 20-ship limit for line combat hulls is enforced for player and AI via
  possible_construction + scripted trigger cp26_under_reduced_fleet_military_cap
  on military ship_sizes. The old ship_data pattern (factor = 0 when
  count_owned_ship >= 15 or >= 6 with cp26_is_non_hero_military_ship) was removed
  from country_types as redundant UI-less duplication. Early corvette “soft”
  throttles described below in bullets 1–2 are partially superseded for the hard
  cap; technology-based fraction modifiers remain.

Main conclusion
---------------
The best implementation is not a brutal purge event and not a vanilla resource
economy rewrite. Infinite already has a native empire-wide pressure system:
officers. The coherent model is:

1. AI stops preferring new military ships once it already owns about 18 military
   ships, with the design target still being around 15 in normal play.
2. Small hulls are suppressed earlier, at about 8 military ships, so the AI
   naturally shifts toward fewer heavier hulls when technology allows it.
3. If scripted spawns or war losses still create edge cases, the officers system
   makes oversized fleets under-crewed and weak instead of deleting ships.
4. Crises and factions are compacted at their scripted spawn source.

Why not naval capacity
----------------------
Infinite has naval-capacity logic mostly removed or commented out. The vanilla
alloys budget still contains references to naval-capacity conditions, but the
important "over naval capacity" spending logic is commented. Rebuilding the
system through ai_budget or resource costs is risky and can destabilize
construction queues. That path was rejected.

ai_budget = crash confirmé : tentatives passées de scripter un soft cap via
common/ai_budget/*.txt ont produit des SIGSEGV dans CConstructionQueueManager::
DailyUpdate et CSaveGameTaskManager::CreateMemoryFiles. La queue de
construction interne du moteur ne supporte pas qu'un script lui dicte de
"vendre des alloys au-dessus du cap" — elle déréfère un pointeur invalide
quand un AI sous-priorise un building. Voir CP26_CONSTRUCTION_QUEUE_CRASH_FIX
pour le pattern.

Donc la stratégie EFFECTIVE est :
   - Pas de manipulation de naval_cap.
   - Pas d'ai_budget custom.
   - PRESSION OFFICIERS : le système natif d'Infinite suffit. Les ships
     consomment des officiers (variable d'empire affichée en top-bar). Une
     flotte sous-officiée tire moins (jusqu'à -125% fire rate clampé à 0%
     sans officiers, voir defines OFFICERS_MAX_FIRE_RATE_PENALTY). L'AI
     préfère donc maintenir des flottes compactes correctement crewées
     plutôt que d'empiler des hulls vides.
   - COMMAND_LIMIT plus bas : limite per-fleet à 18 (base) + 27 (max bonus
     amiral) = 45 absolu. Force la fragmentation en plusieurs flottes ou
     l'arrêt de production.
   - CAPS ship_data : factor=0 dans le ship_data quand l'AI a déjà 15+ ships
     militaires (cible design alignée 2026-05-14). Pas un hard cap (l'AI
     peut overshoot en chantier), mais une pression de préférence forte.
   - DOCTRINES de combat : 4 minor factions sont plus puissantes par hull
     (Gorn lourd, Tholian shields, Orion punchy, Breen armor) → quantité
     compensée par qualité.
   - SPAWNS SCRIPTÉS COMPACTÉS : crises (Borg/Prethoryn/Khan), marauders,
     mercenary, faction start fleets — réduits à la source.

Le tout = soft cap multi-couches sans toucher au moteur de queue.

Why not command limit alone
---------------------------
Command limit exists, but it is a per-fleet limit. Lowering it alone can make
the AI split the same number of ships into more fleets. It is useful as a
supporting bound, but it is not an empire-wide hull cap.

Why officers are the correct native system
------------------------------------------
The game already tracks officers in the top bar:
- interface/main.gui uses tb_officers_group.
- interface/resource_groups/topbar_single_resource_groups.txt defines the
  officers resource group.
- fleet_view.gui and fleet_manager_view.gui expose prioritize_officers.

If the top bar shows +6 but the current officer stock remains 0, that is not
necessarily a bug: new officers can be immediately assigned to active ships.
The meaningful checks are the top-bar tooltip and each fleet's officer fill.

Expected gameplay result
------------------------
- AI empires should build fewer ships and stop actively preferring more military
  hulls around 18 ships, while normal pressure should keep them closer to the
  intended 15-ish scale.
- The AI should naturally replace losses with heavier ships once tech unlocks
  them, because small hulls are suppressed earlier.
- The AI is not expected to proactively delete a corvette just to build a
  heavier hull. The engine does not expose a reliable safe "delete small ship
  to upgrade composition" behavior in the audited files. Because of this, the
  medium/heavy build stop uses 18 rather than a hard 15, while small hulls are
  still suppressed at 8.
- Oversized fleets can still happen through events, gifts, crisis scripts or
  saves already in progress, but they should be weaker if officers are short.
- Hero ships are not counted separately by the AI ship_data checks. Fully
  exempting hero ships from officer needs would require custom hero component
  variants or deeper design overrides. They are, however, excluded from the AI
  hull-count suppression through cp26_is_non_hero_military_ship.

UI status
---------
- The officers display already exists in the vanilla top bar.
- A direct "current military ships / max military ships" top-bar counter was not
  implemented. The audited UI exposes resources cleanly, but not an obvious safe
  binding for a scripted ship-count display in the top bar.
- Recommended visible proxy for now: top-bar officers + fleet officer fill.

Known constraints and risks
---------------------------
- This is a soft/native cap model, not an engine-enforced hard cap.
- A saved game with existing oversized AI fleets will not instantly shrink.
- Scripted-effect overrides can create duplicate-definition log warnings. These
  are expected when CP26 intentionally replaces vanilla/Infinite effects.
- Endgame crisis balance still needs live testing because "fewer but stronger"
  crisis waves can feel too spiky or too weak depending on galaxy year and
  difficulty.
- Officer cost is built from both ship sizes and components, so exact per-ship
  officer math can vary by design loadout.

Recommended test protocol
-------------------------
1. Start a fresh test game.
2. Confirm no construction-queue crash on unpause and first monthly tick.
3. Confirm officers appear in the top bar and fleet officer fill changes when
   ships are created.
4. Let the game run until several AI empires unlock medium/heavy hulls.
5. Check AI military ship counts:
   - small hull spam should stop early.
   - total military count should hover much closer to 15 than before.
   - scripted crises/factions should spawn fewer objects than vanilla scale.
6. Review error.log after the test. Expected: possible duplicate override
   warnings. Not expected: construction queue, invalid event target or missing
   ship design crashes.

Final recommendation
--------------------
This is the right direction: compact AI construction + compact scripted spawns +
native officer pressure. It matches Infinite's existing systems and avoids the
two dangerous paths: deleting AI ships by script or rewriting vanilla resource
budgets.
